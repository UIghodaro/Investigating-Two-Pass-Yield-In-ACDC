THE ~10-EDGE RECALL CEILING FINDING
------------------------------------------------------------------------

At τ=0.067, approximately 10 ground truth edges are unrecovered by EVERY
condition — including zero_ablation (200 edges, no corruption signal at all).
Evidence:
  - zero_ablation: 26 TP, 11 FN  (recall = 0.703)
  - random_random:  26 TP, 11 FN  (recall = 0.703)
  - random_answer_doc + random_random union: 27 TP, 10 FN
    → random_answer_doc contributes 1 GT edge not found by random_random
  - No union reaches more than 27 TP

Interpretation: these ~10 edges have KL contributions that fall below
τ=0.067 regardless of how the counterfactual distribution is constructed.
They are structurally unrecoverable at this threshold. This is a genuine
empirical finding — it identifies a hard limit of ACDC at the standard
Conmy (2023) threshold for this task.

------------------------------------------------------------------------
TWO-PASS VALUE ASSESSMENT (naive union)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Two-pass naive union does NOT meaningfully beat the best single-pass.
This is a valid (negative) finding, not a failure:
  - The best single-pass (random_random) already saturates recall at τ=0.067
  - Naive union mostly accumulates FPs rather than recovering new GT edges
  - This motivates the marginal_gain approach: smarter union that filters FPs

The two-pass value proposition is fundamentally threshold-dependent:
  - At τ=0.067: single-pass recall ceiling ~0.703, little room for improvement
  - At HIGHER thresholds: circuits are smaller/more conservative, so two-pass
    unions would have more room to show recall gains
  - A suite at τ=0.1 or τ=0.15 could tell a very different story

THE SUBSET RELATIONSHIP: random_random ⊆ zero_ablation ACROSS THRESHOLDS
------------------------------------------------------------------------

At τ=0.067 the subset was perfect: Jaccard = 39/200 = 0.195, meaning all 39
random_random edges were contained in zero_ablation's 200.

Solving J = x / (|A| + |B| - x) for intersection size x at each threshold:

  τ=0.067: x = 39/200 * (39+200-x) → x ≈ 39  (perfect subset: 39/39)
  τ=0.085: J=0.179, |A|=36, |B|=195  → x ≈ 35  (near-subset: 35/36, 1 edge outside)
  τ=0.10:  J=0.161, |A|=33, |B|=191  → x ≈ 31  (near-subset: 31/33, 2 edges outside)

The 2 random_random edges outside zero_ablation at τ=0.10 are almost certainly FPs
(random_random has 7 FPs at τ=0.10 and they likely only surface under corrupted ACDC).

IMPLICATION FOR MARGINAL_GAIN: At τ=0.10, zero_ablation has 31 TPs and
random_random has 26 TPs. The intersection of ~31 edges contains at most 26 TPs
(all of random_random's). This means zero_ablation's 5 extra TPs are exclusively
in zero_ablation \ random_random — exactly the H_B \ H_A candidate set that
marginal_gain evaluates. The hypothesis is confirmed.

------------------------------------------------------------------------
CROSS-THRESHOLD JACCARD TRENDS
------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. RANDOM_RANDOM / ZERO_ABLATION DIVERGES WITH THRESHOLD (0.195→0.179→0.161).
   As threshold rises, random_random sheds FPs faster than zero_ablation. The
   subset relationship weakens (39/39 → 35/36 → 31/33) but remains near-perfect.
   Interpretation: the corruption signal in random_random progressively filters
   out FPs that zero_ablation (no corruption signal) retains at any threshold.

2. RANDOM_DEF / RANDOM_DEF_DOC JUMPS THEN STABILISES (0.478→0.667→0.667).
   Both conditions hit perfect precision (1.0) at τ=0.085 simultaneously and
   converge to the same 12–13 edge circuit. Their Jaccard stabilises because the
   circuits themselves stabilise. The jump from 0.478 to 0.667 reflects that
   both shed their different FP sets while retaining the same TPs.

3. RANDOM_ANSWER / RANDOM_ANSWER_DOC IS STABLE (0.696→0.652→0.700).
   Family similarity is robust to threshold — these circuits remain ~70% identical
   across the full range. Running both in a two-pass is redundant at any threshold.

4. RANDOM_DOC / VL_RANDOM_DOC CONVERGES TO NEAR-IDENTITY AT τ=0.10 (J=0.875).
   At τ=0.067 these shared half their edges (J=0.500). By τ=0.10 both have shed
   most FPs and the remaining circuits are almost the same 7-edge structure.
   These two corruptions are effectively redundant at high thresholds.

5. THE RQ2 FINDING IS THRESHOLD-ROBUST.
   Low Jaccard ≠ useful complementarity holds at all three thresholds.
   The distinctness between conditions remains in FP space, not GT space.
   This conclusion does not depend on which threshold was used for Stage 1.

POSSIBLE ALTERNATIVE NARRATIVES / ANGLES FOR DISSERTATION
------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. RQ1 (yield) is the strongest contribution:
   - 8× systematic empirical comparison of corruption strategies is novel
   - Conmy (2023) used only one corruption — this work extends that
   - Finding that vary_length is useless and random_random dominates
     is not predictable from first principles; it required running the suite
   - The precision/recall profile diversity (random_doc vs random_random)
     is itself an interesting structural result

2. The hard recall ceiling is an interesting secondary finding:
   - ~10 GT edges unreachable at τ=0.067 regardless of corruption
   - This is a property of the threshold, not the corruption family
   - A threshold sensitivity analysis (higher τ) would test this directly

3. marginal_gain.py (not yet implemented) may still produce a positive result:
   - Even at τ=0.067, a smart union filtering FPs could beat random_random
   - random_answer_doc contributes 1 extra GT edge; marginal_gain could
     potentially recover that edge while reducing the 13 FPs in random_random

4. Higher threshold experiment:
   - At τ > 0.067, each single-pass circuit would be smaller/more conservative
   - Two-pass unions would have more room to show recall gains
   - The complementarity picture (RQ2) may be more interesting if no single
     corruption dominates at a higher threshold

1. CORRUPTION FAMILY DETERMINES CIRCUIT OVERLAP MORE THAN INDIVIDUAL CHOICE.
   random_answer and random_answer_doc produce nearly identical circuits (J=0.696).
   Running both in a two-pass is largely redundant. The "_doc" compound variants

   random_doc vs random_answer (J=0.087) are very different circuits — but
   in the union analysis, random_doc adds 0 new GT edges over random_random.

   random_random's entire 39-edge circuit is a strict subset of zero_ablation
   (Jaccard = 39/200 = 0.195). Yet random_random has far better precision
   (0.667 vs 0.130). The corruption signal filters the zero_ablation circuit
   down to a more focused subgraph — this is an elegant framing of what
   corruption-based ACDC actually does.

DISSERTATION FRAMING NOTES
------------------------------------------------------------------------

- Negative results are valid and publishable. "Naive union two-pass does not
  improve over best single-pass at standard threshold" is a finding, not a flaw.
- The risk of criticism: "could have been predicted without the suite" — defence:
  (a) the yield ranking is empirical, not obvious; (b) the magnitude of differences
  (vary_length: 0 edges vs random_random: F1=0.684) could not be predicted;
  (c) the hard recall ceiling is a genuine discovery
- The strongest framing: this work answers whether systematisable heuristics
  over dataset corruption affect circuit quality. They do (RQ1). Whether they
  enable two-pass improvement is threshold-dependent (RQ3 partial/negative).

STAGE 3b — MARGINAL GAIN SUITE RESULTS AND DIAGNOSIS

Source: runs/marginal_gain_suite_SUITE_20260324_041413_docstring.json
τ=0.10, device=cpu, seed=0, all-pairs from SUITE_20260324_041413

HEADLINE: Marginal gain (original algorithm) fails to recover the
5 extra GT edges that zero_ablation holds over random_random.

Key results:
- Improvements only occur when zero_ablation is H_B (the candidate pool).
- In those cases, exactly ONE edge is kept across all H_A conditions:
    ('blocks.3.hook_resid_post', (None,), 'blocks.0.hook_resid_pre', (None,))
  KL improvement: 0.45–0.61 depending on H_A. NOT a GT edge — it is the
  direct embedding→final-output residual skip connection (a FP).
- Consequence: Mode B F1 DECREASES when this edge is added (1 FP added,
  0 TPs added). The one edge marginal gain finds is a Mode A improvement
  but a Mode B regression.
- For all non-zero_ablation H_B pairs: improvement = exactly 0.0 for every
  candidate edge. This is not a near-miss — it is literally zero.

PATH CONNECTIVITY FAILURE (root cause):
The receiver hook in TLACDCExperiment sets the activation at any receiver
node to zero (zero ablation) and then adds back the clean activation for
each PRESENT incoming edge. If a candidate edge E adds a new sender to
receiver R, but R's OUTPUT edges are not present in H_A, the change to
R's activation never propagates to the final output. KL = exactly 0.0.
This affects every candidate edge whose receiver has no active downstream
path in H_A. For small circuits like random_answer (14 edges), almost all
H_B candidates are in this situation.

CONFIRMED ABLATION-AGNOSTIC (2026-03-27):
marginal_gain.py was re-run with --corruption random_random (resample
ablation: absent edges replaced with activations from random_random
corrupted inputs rather than zeros). Result: identical failure pattern —
improvements exactly 0.0 for all candidates except the embed→output skip
connection FP. The path connectivity failure is a structural property of
edge-level testing, not an artefact of the ablation method. Whether absent
downstream edges are zeroed or set to corrupted activations, they are still
REPLACED — so the candidate edge's effect never propagates past the first
absent downstream edge.
Output: analysis/stage3b_marginal_gain/
  marginal_gain_random-random_zero-ablation_resample-random-random_SUITE_20260324_041413_docstring.json

THE PRUNING DIRECTION IS CIRCULAR:
Since random_random ⊆ zero_ablation, H_A ∪ H_B = zero_ablation. Pruning
from the naive union at τ=0.10 is equivalent to re-running ACDC on
zero_ablation at τ=0.10 — which is how zero_ablation was built. This
approach recovers zero_ablation, not a refined version of it.

HEAD 1.2 — THE KEY STRUCTURAL FINDING
------------------------------------------------------------------------

The 6 GT edges that zero_ablation uniquely recovers (not in random_random):

  ('blocks.1.attn.hook_k', (None, None, 2), 'blocks.1.hook_k_input', (None, None, 2))
  ('blocks.1.attn.hook_q', (None, None, 2), 'blocks.1.hook_q_input', (None, None, 2))
  ('blocks.1.hook_k_input', (None, None, 2), 'blocks.0.attn.hook_result', (None, None, 5))
  ('blocks.1.hook_k_input', (None, None, 2), 'blocks.0.hook_resid_pre', (None,))
  ('blocks.1.hook_q_input', (None, None, 2), 'blocks.0.attn.hook_result', (None, None, 5))
  ('blocks.1.hook_q_input', (None, None, 2), 'blocks.0.hook_resid_pre', (None,))

These are ALL part of head 1.2's Q and K computation path. Verified:
random_random has ZERO edges involving head 1.2 in any role — not as
receiver, not as sender, not downstream. Head 1.2 is completely absent
from random_random's circuit.

WHY zero_ablation finds head 1.2 but random_random doesn't:
- Zero ablation asks: "does this edge contribute anything at all?"
  Head 1.2 does compute something → passes.
- Random_random asks: "does this edge contribute something specifically
  beyond what a random example would provide?"
  Head 1.2's Q/K attention pattern is apparently similar whether the
  input is a real docstring or a random one → fails.
  Head 1.2 is STRUCTURALLY ACTIVE but not DISCRIMINATIVELY ACTIVE
  under corruption-based ACDC. No corruption method finds it.

WHY the original paper found head 1.2:
Heimersheim & Janiak (2022) used MANUAL NODE-LEVEL activation patching,
not ACDC. They patched entire head outputs and observed the effect.
Head 1.2 passes the node-level test because its collective contribution
is significant — but individual edges fail ACDC's edge-level threshold
because the contribution is distributed. ACDC's edge-level granularity
is the source of the miss, not a flaw in the corruption choice.

KEY IMPLICATION: Any multi-corruption consensus approach (including
Garcia-Carrasco's method applied here) would ALSO miss head 1.2, since
no corruption stresses it. Only zero ablation or manual node-level
patching can detect it.

LITERATURE FINDINGS — COMBINATION METHODS IN PRIOR WORK
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Heimersheim & Janiak (2022/2023):
"A circuit for Python docstrings in a 4-layer attention-only transformer"
- Single corruption only (disjoint variable names ≈ random_random).
- Manual node-level activation patching, NOT ACDC.
- No combination procedure — single pass, manual inspection.
- The "augmented circuit" additions were heuristic observations.
- Ground truth (37 edges) established manually, predates ACDC.

Garcia-Carrasco et al. (2024):
"How does GPT-2 Predict Acronyms?" (AISTATS 2024)
- Used THREE corruption types diagnostically:
  (i) corrupt current word, (ii) corrupt previous words,
  (iii) corrupt previous predicted letters.
- Each corruption illuminated a DIFFERENT ASPECT of information flow.
- Combination method: QUALITATIVE MANUAL SYNTHESIS — no formal rule.
  No union/intersection/voting. Expert judgment assembled the final
  8-head circuit from patterns visible across corruption conditions.
- Validation: mean ablation (NOT the same as the discovery corruption).

NOVELTY CONFIRMATION: No prior work formally compares naive union vs
threshold-filtered combination for multi-corruption circuit discovery.
This project is the first formal treatment of multi-corruption
combination, and the Stage 3 comparison (naive union vs marginal gain)
has no direct precedent in the cited literature.

THE GT IS UNDERSPECIFIED AT THE EDGE LEVEL — MODE B LIMITATION
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Heimersheim & Janiak's 37-edge GT was established via manual NODE-LEVEL
activation patching. They confirmed head 1.2 is in the circuit by patching
its entire output and observing a performance drop. Their 37 edges capture
which information flows INTO each key head (e.g., the Q/K inputs to head
1.2 from head 0.5 and the embedding), but they do NOT enumerate where each
head's output flows DOWNSTREAM.

Consequence: the GT contains no outgoing edges from hook_result[1,2]. Yet
in ACDC's edge-level representation, hook_result[1,2] must connect to some
downstream receiver (e.g., hook_q_input[3,0]) for head 1.2 to form a
connected path to the output. These downstream edges are present in
zero_ablation's circuit and are kept by marginal bundle gain's refinement
step — but they are labelled FP by Mode B because H&J never annotated them.

These are NOT false positives in any mechanistic sense. They are necessary
connectivity edges that the GT simply does not cover because the GT was
built at a different level of granularity than ACDC's edge representation.

IMPLICATION FOR MODE B EVALUATION:
Mode B evaluation against H&J's 37-edge GT has a systematic blind spot for
any circuit that correctly recovers head 1.2: the downstream connectivity
edges will always be counted as FPs, suppressing precision scores even when
the circuit is mechanistically correct. This makes Mode B an unreliable
evaluator for marginal bundle gain circuits specifically.

The correct response is to treat Mode B as indicative but not definitive
for Stage 3b, and to weight Mode A (circuit efficiency under zero ablation)
as the primary evaluation for the marginal bundle gain circuit.

PROPOSED MODE A EFFICIENCY COMPARISON:
To compare circuit quality in a way that does not depend on the GT's
edge-level completeness, evaluate all circuits under a common ablation
(zero ablation, corruption-agnostic) and measure KL divergence vs the
full unablated model. A circuit that perfectly recovers the model's
computation would have KL=0. Comparing:
  1. KL of the 37-edge GT circuit under zero ablation
  2. KL of random_random single-pass circuit (33 edges)
  3. KL of marginal bundle gain circuit (various τ)
would reveal whether our circuits approach or exceed the GT circuit's
functional completeness — without penalising them for including connectivity
edges that the GT omits. This comparison is directly meaningful against
Conmy et al. and H&J: if our circuit has lower KL than their manually
constructed GT, we have evidence that the two-pass procedure recovers a
more complete circuit. Implementation: evaluate each circuit as a fixed edge
set using the zero-ablation Mode A infrastructure already in marginal_gain.py.

STAGE 3b REVISED ALGORITHM — MARGINAL BUNDLE GAIN
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Motivation: head 1.2's Q/K sub-circuit cannot be recovered by individual
edge testing (path connectivity failure) but CAN be tested as a unit
(a "bundle" = all edges belonging to one attention head). This mirrors
how Heimersheim & Janiak originally found head 1.2: at the head level,
not the edge level.

Algorithm:
1. Group zero_ablation edges into head-level bundles.
   Bundle for head (L, N) = all edges where receiver ∈
   {hook_q_input[L,N], hook_k_input[L,N], hook_v_input[L,N],
    hook_q[L,N], hook_k[L,N], hook_v[L,N]}
   PLUS edges where sender = hook_result[L,N].
2. For each bundle B not already covered by random_random:
   - Set circuit = random_random ∪ B
   - Use random_random's corrupted cache for absent edges
     (NOT zero ablation — tests whether the bundle is task-specifically
     non-random, using H_A's own corruption criterion)
   - If KL(random_random) - KL(random_random ∪ B) > τ: accept bundle
3. Optional refinement: within accepted bundles, test individual edge
   removal to prune FPs.
4. Final circuit = random_random ∪ {accepted bundle edges, post-refinement}

Why random_random's corruption (not zero ablation) for the bundle test:
- Zero ablation asks "does this bundle compute anything?" — too permissive,
  same problem as before.
- random_random corruption asks "is this bundle specifically task-relevant
  beyond random examples in the context of H_A?" — meaningful test.
- If head 1.2 passes, it means: in the context of random_random's circuit,
  head 1.2 Q/K provides discriminative value. This is a genuine finding.

Framing: "Marginal bundle gain under shared corruption ablation" —
validates zero_ablation's sub-circuits using random_random's ablation
criterion. Formally analogous to Garcia-Carrasco's multi-corruption
synthesis but with an explicit KL threshold in place of expert judgment.
Implementation: marginal_bundle_gain.py (written and run — see results above).

Interpretation: With zero ablation, a small partial circuit activates a handful

stream that is missing the contributions of every upstream head that was supposed

diverge MORE from the full model than a uniformly-zeroed residual stream. The empty
circuit is internally consistent (all zeros); small partial circuits are not.

Why mbg09 differs: by including head 1.2's complete Q/K/V sub-circuit (all incoming
and outgoing edges), mbg09 provides a coherent computational unit that has enough
context to produce outputs closer to the full model. This validates the bundle-level
testing approach — the relevant unit is the whole head, not individual edges.

This is the strongest Mode A argument for marginal bundle gain:
  "GT and random_random circuits, under zero ablation, perform worse than the
   empty circuit. mbg09, by including head 1.2's complete sub-circuit, is the
   smallest circuit that beats the empty baseline. Circuit completeness at the
   head level — not edge count — determines whether partial circuits recover or
   degrade model behaviour under ablation."

ZERO_ABLATION THRESHOLD ANOMALY AND DISSERTATION FRAMING
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Full zero_ablation threshold sweep:

  τ        Edges   TPs   Recall
  --------------------------------
  0.067     200     26    0.703
  0.085     195     31    0.838
  0.095     158     25    0.676   ← anomaly
  0.100     191     31    0.838

At τ=0.095, zero_ablation drops to 158 edges and 25 TPs — fewer than both
neighbouring thresholds and BELOW random_random's 26 TPs. This was confirmed
by rerunning: ACDC is fully deterministic at seed=0 on CPU and reproduced
the identical result. The anomaly is stable, not a stochastic artefact.

ROOT CAUSE: ACDC's greedy sequential pruning is path-dependent. At τ=0.095,
some early edge pruned at this threshold (but kept at both τ=0.085 and τ=0.10)
changes the circuit context for downstream edges, causing a cascade that removes
more edges than expected — including TPs. Zero_ablation is more susceptible to
this than corruption-based conditions because it has no corruption signal to
stabilise the search; every edge evaluation is noisier.

This is not unique to our implementation — it is a known property of greedy
path-dependent algorithms and can be observed in ACDC generally.

DISSERTATION FRAMING (recommended one-sentence treatment):
"Conmy's threshold (τ=0.095) was used to establish direct comparability for
Stage 1. However, zero_ablation — which provides the complementary high-recall
circuit necessary for Stage 2/3 analysis — exhibited path-dependent instability
at this threshold (158 edges, recall=0.676, falling below both τ=0.085 and
τ=0.10). Stage 2/3 analysis therefore uses τ=0.10, where zero_ablation behaves
consistently (191 edges, recall=0.838)."
